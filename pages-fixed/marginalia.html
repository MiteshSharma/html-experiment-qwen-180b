§ 01 — On the Craft

Before the eye can read, the eye feels.

Typography is the craft of endowing human language with a durable visual form. It lives in the tension between the mark and the meaning, between the ink and the white it sits upon — a discipline that rewards patience, precision, and a certain reverence for the blank page.

Marginalia is devoted to that discipline. Across four sections we trace the anatomy of a letter, meet the faces that shape how we read, and return to the room where a page is first imagined. Every issue is set by hand, proofed until it breathes, and printed to be held.

§ 03 — A Study

The Anatomy ofa Letterform

Every glyph is a small architecture. Learn its parts and you will never read a page the same way again. Hover the specimen.

BowlThe closed, rounded stroke of a curved letter — the belly of a b, the heart of an o.
StemThe main vertical stroke; the column a letter stands upon.
CounterThe enclosed space within a bowl or stroke — where white breathes inside black.
SerifThe finishing stroke at the end of a stem; a small foot that guides the eye along a line.
X-heightThe height of a lowercase x — the quiet measure that sets how light or dense a face feels.
KerningThe fine tuning of space between two specific letters, until they sit together without argument.

§ 04 — Notes

Notes from
the Reading Room

A well-set page is a held breath.

There is a moment, just before reading, when the whole sentence is visible at once — a chord rather than a sequence. Typography is the art of controlling that moment: of giving the eye a rhythm to fall into, a pace to keep, a reason to linger at the edge of the page.

The margins are not empty. They are the silence between notes, the white that lets the black mean something. To set a page well is to compose that silence as carefully as the words themselves.

§ 06 — The Colophon

Colophon & Marginalia

This issue was composed entirely in the browser and printed on a warm, uncoated stock. It is set in three voices — a display serif for the headlines, a grotesque for the machinery, and a book serif for the body — chosen to argue, gently, with one another across the spread.

Marginalia is published twice a year by a small independent studio that believes reading should be a pleasure of the eye before it is a task of the mind. Every proof is read aloud. Every margin is measured twice.

The notes in the margin are where the reader meets the writer — a place for argument, doubt, and the occasional second thought.
